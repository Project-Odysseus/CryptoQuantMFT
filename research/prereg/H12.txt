PRE-REGISTRATION H12: THE TAKER BASKET'S RETURN COMES FROM WHICH COINS IT FAVOURS, SO A SLOW VERSION KEEPS IT AT LESS TURNOVER
Version 1. Written 2026-10-12, before any H12 test was run. Changes after results = a new version (lock it again), logged
as extra trials.

PRIOR EXPOSURE (what has already been looked at that bears on this)
- The taker-buy basket (7-day share, long the top fifth, short the bottom fifth) has been studied on all of this
  data: 2026-09-26 (8 features, two universes), 2026-09-26 robustness (45 configurations: universe 20/30/50, legs
  10/20/30%, rebalance every 3/5/7/10/14 days), 2026-09-29 (Kraken prices through the engine), 2026-10-03 (its two
  legs), 2026-10-04 (signal lab and H6), 2026-10-05 (H9, a rank buffer, killed). The baseline's numbers on this
  exact window are known from H9: turnover 51.4 times a year, net return +18.4%, Sharpe 0.72, +11.3% at 2x costs.
- 2026-10-11, signal card of the 7-day ranking (the reason for this test): IC +0.054 at 7 days, but time-shifted
  copies of the ranking average +0.066, and the share measured against each coin's own history has an IC of -0.016.
  The card's reading: the ranking favours the same coins throughout and those did better; the weekly changes in it
  are not shown to predict anything.
- Evidence already seen that points against this hypothesis: the robustness grid found 10-14 days best and nothing
  slower was run; H9 found that a 10-day schedule replaces most of the basket at each re-rank (turnover 51 times a
  year) and that holding coins a little longer (the buffer) cost 5 points of return a year. If the return came only
  from a fixed set of coins, the buffer should have been harmless.
- Not run before: any score longer than 7 days, any rebalance slower than 14 days, any split of the score into a
  slow part and a fast part.
- So the level of the basket's return is known and is not what is tested. What is tested is the difference a slow
  score and a slow schedule make on the same data. A relative result on data the base rule was chosen on is weaker
  than a test on new data.

MARKET BEHAVIOUR
Some coins have a persistently higher share of aggressive buying in their volume than others, and those coins have
done better than the ones with a persistently low share. The differences between coins last for months; the week-to-
week changes in a coin's share carry no further information about its next return.

MECHANISM
A persistently high share of aggressive buying marks a coin that is being accumulated by buyers who accept paying the
spread, month after month; a persistently low share marks one that holders are leaving. That is a property of the
coin over a long stretch, not of one week. The other side is whoever supplies liquidity in those coins and is paid
the spread for it. No claim is made that this persists outside the window; the mechanism is weak and the result is
read with that in mind.

OBSERVABLE PREDICTION AND EXPECTED SIGN
A basket ranked on the 90-day share and rebalanced every 30 days trades less than half as much as the paper book's
rule and its net return is not lower. A basket ranked only on the fast part of the score (the 7-day share minus the
90-day share) earns nothing before costs.

DATA
The signal lab's data and costs (src/research/signal_lab.py), as in H9: Binance USDT-perp daily closes, volume,
taker-buy volume and settled funding; the 30 most traded coins whose Kraken perp had opened by that day (point-in-
time listing snapshot); 0.05% fee and slippage by liquidity tier per side; funding on every position. Window: the
first day with at least 10 names (2022-03-23) to 2025-12-31. The frozen holdout (2026 on) stays locked.

SIGNAL CARD (run first, logged as one trial)
The card of the slow score ("taker-buy share, 90 days", a ranking of coins, 30-day horizon, direction +1, family H12)
is run before the strategy test. If its IC is zero or negative the strategy test is not run and the result is KILL.
The card is expected to say "no evidence" all the same: its placebo asks whether the ranking predicts better at the
right date than at other dates, and this hypothesis says it does not. The gate is therefore passed by hand:
SIGNAL CARD OVERRIDE: the hypothesis is that the ranking is a fixed tilt between coins, which the card's time-shift placebo fails by construction; the owner approved this test on 2026-10-12 knowing the 7-day card said "no evidence: a fixed tilt".
The override's reason was written by the assistant; the owner's approval was of the test, given after reading that
verdict.

RULE
Scores, each from data up to that day's close:
- fast (the paper book's): taker-buy volume over the last 7 days divided by volume over the last 7 days, minus 0.5
  (at least 5 days of data);
- slow: the same over the last 90 days (at least 60 days of data);
- fast part: the fast score minus the slow score.
Every N days, counted from the window's first day: long the top 20% of the ranked universe by score, short the
bottom 20%, equal weight within a side, half of gross long and half short, 1x gross; flat with fewer than 10 ranked
names. A coin without enough history for the score is not ranked. Between rebalance days the weights are held; a
position is re-trimmed only when it has drifted more than 1% of equity from its weight (the paper book's band).
Decided and traded at the day's close, as in the earlier studies.
- Baseline (the rule in the paper book, H9's baseline): fast score, N = 10.
- Slow basket (primary): slow score, N = 30.

PRIMARY OUTCOME
Two numbers, slow basket against baseline over the whole window at 1x costs: turnover a year, and net return a year.

SECONDARY OUTCOMES
- Sharpe, worst drawdown, costs and funding a year, names per side, for both.
- The difference in daily net returns, annualised, with a 95% interval (circular block bootstrap, 20-day blocks,
  2,000 draws, seed 0).
- Per calendar year, both.
- The two changes one at a time: the slow score with N = 10, and the fast score with N = 30.
- The basket on the fast part of the score (N = 10), before and after costs: the direct test of whether the weekly
  changes carry anything.
- The slow basket with the 180-day share (at least 120 days of data).
- The slow basket with its rebalance days moved by 10 and by 20 days: a 30-day schedule has only 46 rebalance days
  in this window, so the starting day could matter. Reported, not judged.
- The slow basket on today's Kraken list from 2020-06 (the original study's universe). Reported, not judged.
- Two descriptions of the runs above (no new run): the share of names replaced at a rebalance, for the baseline and
  the slow basket; and the slow basket's price P&L by coin (the weight held times the coin's next daily return), as
  the share that comes from its best coin and from its best three.

PARAMETER GRID (every configuration is a trial; count them)
9 runs: the slow basket (primary); the slow basket at 2x costs; slow score with N = 10; fast score with N = 30; the
fast part with N = 10; the 180-day share with N = 30; the slow basket moved by 10 days and by 20 days; the slow basket
on today's list. With the signal card: 10 trials. The baseline at 1x, at 2x and on today's list are H9's runs repeated
as the comparison and are not counted again. Nothing else is run.

PASS CRITERIA (all three)
1. Turnover is at least 50% lower than the baseline's.
2. Net return a year is not more than 1 percentage point below the baseline's.
3. At 2x costs the slow basket's net return is above the baseline's.

KILL IF (any)
- The signal card's IC of the slow score is zero or negative.
- The slow basket's net return is zero or negative.
- Its net return is more than 3 percentage points a year below the baseline's.
Between kill and pass: inconclusive, and the paper book keeps its rule.

WHAT THIS TEST CAN SHOW
In H9 the difference between two versions of this basket had a 95% interval about 9 points a year wide on each side.
The same will hold here: a pass on the point estimates says the slow version was not worse in this window, not that
it is better. The turnover figure, by contrast, is measured almost without noise.

WHAT A PASS LEADS TO
The slow basket as a second basket in a paper book, next to the current one (a 90-day signal added to
src/portfolio/basket.py), so both build a record on the same days. The paper book's own rule does not change on this
result. A pass is also a reason to ask the owner for one look at the frozen 2026 window for both versions.

KNOWN RISKS AND BIASES
- The base rule was selected on this data, so its return here is flattered; only the difference is new.
- A fixed difference between about thirty coins is very few independent observations, however many days it is
  measured over. One or two coins could carry the result; the per-year table is the only guard against that here.
- The 90-day window was chosen from the card's finding on this same data.
- The slow basket holds its coins for months, so it is closer to a static long/short position in a handful of coins:
  less diversified in time than the baseline, and more exposed to a single coin's collapse or delisting.
- Binance prices and funding stand in for Kraken's; delisted Kraken perps are missing.
- One window of under four years.
