PRE-REGISTRATION H14: A CAUSAL THREE-STATE REGIME OVERLAY COMBINES A TREND MODEL AND A RANGE MODEL ON EQUITY INDEX FUTURES BETTER THAN SIMPLER COMBINATIONS
Version 1. Written 2026-10-19 from the owner's specification, before any H14 result existed. Every number is in
research/h14_regime_switch.toml (version 1); the rules are in src/research/regime_switch.py and
src/signals/regime_features.py, tested on synthetic data only. Changes after results = a new version (lock it again),
logged as extra trials. Research and paper only: the owner may not trade these markets personally.

QUESTION
Can two economically distinct return mechanisms - trend following and short-horizon mean reversion around a slow
anchor - be combined by a timely, causal regime overlay, so that the combination beats each model alone, a naive
50/50 blend and a simple hard switch, after costs, without being explained by equity beta or by holding less?

PRIOR EXPOSURE
- H11 (2026-10-09): 4/48-day trend on 12 indices incl. the S&P 500, 2000-2025: equity trend Sharpe 0.01 after costs.
- H13 (2026-10-19): smooth EMA-difference trend on the Nasdaq-100, S&P 500 and STOXX 600, 2005-2025: Sharpe 0.27,
  strategy card "no evidence", timing placebo failed (the return was mostly being long a rising market). So trend on
  US indices has been seen; the range model, the regime overlay and the other markets in this form have not.
- Data checks only (2026-10-19): first/last dates, row counts, duplicates, calendar gaps and close-only history per
  market. No return, signal or chart.
- The specification's parameters are the owner's starting hypotheses, not fitted here.

DATA
- Daily Yahoo index OHLC as the futures proxy (no continuous futures history in the free data): Nasdaq-100 (^NDX, for
  NQ; the development market), S&P 500 (^GSPC, ES), Russell 2000 (^RUT, RTY), DAX (^GDAXI), Euro Stoxx 50 (^STOXX50E),
  Nikkei 225 (^N225), FTSE 100 (^FTSE).
- Return of a rolled future = index return + (flat dividend yield - local 3-month rate, OECD, used a month late) / 252.
  An index has no roll gaps and no back-adjustment, so roll distortions cannot arise; the report says so and checks
  trade concentration around quarterly roll dates anyway. Real NQ data can replace the index later.
- Each market starts the day after its last close-only year (more than 20 bars with high = low = close): DAX 1994,
  Nikkei 1989; isolated such bars are kept. Euro Stoxx 50 exists only from 2007-03.
- Evaluation window 1999-01-04 to 2025-12-31 (the first rate is 1998-01). Exploratory 1999-2011, validation 2012-2025.
  Nothing is fitted on either; both are reported. 2026 is the frozen final holdout and is not looked at.
- Percentile scores: each calendar year scored against the 500 observations before 1 January, frozen within the year
  (walk-forward calibration); at least 250 needed, else warm-up and no trade.

STRATEGIES (each predefined; all in the config)
1 trend alone; 2A/2B range alone (A symmetric, B short signal capped at 0.5); 3A/3B hard switch (trend model when the
trend probability >= 0.50, range model otherwise); 4A/4B three-state with hysteresis, TRANSITION at 0.25/0.25;
5A/5B three-state with hysteresis and the transition schedule (PRIMARY = 5A); 6A/6B static 50/50 always active;
7A 200-day-MA regime filter (trend model above, range model below); 8 buy-and-hold 1x and a volatility-managed long;
9A exposure-matched static (|5A target| x sign of the 6A signal).
Diagnostics and references, also predefined: 5C no range shorts; 5A without the rapid-breakout exception;
P probability-weighted (trend weight = trend probability: confidence scaling without states); simple filters M2-M6
(|40-day return| > 10%, ADX > 20 and > 25, ER above its median, 20-day MA above 100-day MA, volatility expansion with
a recent breakout) as hard switches; the overlay without each of its five features, and with each feature alone;
an MA-filtered long.
Resolutions of the specification's open points, fixed here: "trend score strongly negative/positive" = trend signal
beyond -/+0.5; the volatility-breakout veto = 10-day vol above 1.5 x 60-day vol; "conflicting component signals" =
at least two scores >= 0.65 and at least two <= 0.35; "directional confirmation" = ER, |slope| or breakout score above
0.5; with confirmed volatility expansion TREND needs 2 confirmations instead of 3; the slow MA is 100 days (section 2).
Positions: target signal x 10% / EWMA volatility (cap 2x), a 2% band, decided at the close of t, traded at the close
of t+1, earning from t+2. Costs per side 1.5 bps (NDX, SPX), 2 (DAX, Euro Stoxx 50, FTSE), 3 (RUT, Nikkei), plus 4
rolls a year at 1 bp; cost cases x0.5, x1, x2.5. Stress rules and the dead zone apply to every combination, not to
the standalone models or the long references.

SENSITIVITY (one at a time around the baseline, then fast and slow joint settings; 18 configurations of 5A)
Regime horizon 30/60; TREND entry 0.60/0.70; RANGE entry 0.30/0.40; confirmation 2/5; range |z| entry 1.25/2.0;
breakout 40/100; anchor 30/60; MA pairs 10/50 and 40/200; fast and slow joint settings.

PRIMARY OUTCOME
5A's daily net returns on the Nasdaq-100, 1999-2025, base costs: the strategy card (src/research/strategy_checks.py,
standard v1) with 6A as the baseline, every NDX configuration as a variant, and its positions for the timing placebo.

OUTCOME CLASSIFICATION (mechanical; Sharpe = net annualised Sharpe at base costs, NDX unless stated)
E1 significant: 5A's card verdict is "weak" or "evidence of an edge".
E2 beats simpler combinations: 5A's Sharpe exceeds 6A's and 3A's in both the exploratory and the validation period,
   and the paired bootstrap interval of 5A minus 6A is above zero over the whole window.
E3 not exposure reduction: 5A's Sharpe exceeds 9A's in both periods.
E4 beats simple filters: 5A's Sharpe is at least the best of 7A and M2-M6 over the whole window.
E5 generalises: 5A's Sharpe exceeds 6A's on the S&P 500 and on at least 3 of the other 5 markets, and the pooled
   (equal-weight across all 7 markets) 5A beats the pooled 6A.
E6 robust: net return positive in at least 80% of the 18 sensitivity configurations, and above 6A's in at least 2/3.
E7 not one episode: 5A's Sharpe without its best calendar year is above zero.
Then, in this order:
C Mostly exposure management: 5A's Sharpe exceeds 6A's over the whole window but E3 fails.
D Not supported: 5A's whole-window Sharpe is at most the larger of 6A's and 3A's, or E4 fails.
A Supported: E1 to E7 all hold.
B Partially supported: E1 and E2 hold but some of E3-E7 fail.
E Inconclusive: anything else (5A ahead on point estimates without the significance to say so).

TRIALS
Every strategy configuration run is a trial: about 37 on the Nasdaq-100, 18 sensitivity configurations, 2 extra cost
cases, 15 per cross-market market. The study counts and logs the exact number.

WHAT EACH RESULT LEADS TO
A or B: owner's call on a paper book with the unchanged rules (research only), after one approved look at 2026.
C, D, E: nothing is built. A post-hoc modification, if any, is a new version, labelled post-hoc, tested separately.

KNOWN RISKS AND BIASES
- Index closes stand in for futures; dividend yields are flat assumptions; costs are assumptions.
- One development market, highly correlated with the cross-markets; few independent regime episodes in 27 years.
- The owner's starting parameters may reflect general knowledge of these markets' history.
- Many diagnostics are reported; only the classification above decides.
