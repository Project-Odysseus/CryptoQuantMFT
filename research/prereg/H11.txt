PRE-REGISTRATION H11: TREND, CARRY AND RELATIVE MOMENTUM ON EQUITY INDEX FUTURES AND CURRENCIES EARN A POSITIVE RETURN AFTER COSTS
Version 1. Written 2026-10-09 before any price, return or signal of these markets was looked at in this project.
Changes after results = a new version (lock it again), logged as extra trials.

PURPOSE
A proof of concept for a multi-asset fund: equity index futures and currencies only (bonds and commodities are outside
the mandate). The fund already runs technical indicators on futures with a discretionary overlay, and trend followers
on bonds. So the book tested here is half directional (trend) and half relative (long some markets, short others),
and the relative half is the part the fund does not have.

PRIOR EXPOSURE
- None in this project: only the first and last dates and the row counts of each series were read (2026-10-09), to
  choose the window. No price, return or chart.
- The ideas come from published work, which is itself a selection: time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen,
  2012), value and momentum across markets (Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen, 2013), carry (Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen
  and Vrugt, 2018). Those samples end between 2009 and 2012; most of the window here after 2012 is new to them.
- The moving-average rule (4 and 48 days) was chosen on BTC, ETH and SOL in this project, not on these markets.

MARKET BEHAVIOUR AND MECHANISM
- Trend: markets that have risen keep rising for some months, because news is absorbed slowly and investors adjust
  positions gradually. The other side is whoever takes profit early or hedges.
- Carry: currencies with high interest rates do not fall by enough to cancel the rate difference. The return pays for
  the risk of sharp losses when risk appetite drops.
- Relative momentum: the markets that did best over the last year keep doing better than the rest for the next month.

DATA (research/data/multi_asset_markets.csv lists every market)
- 12 equity indices, daily closes from Yahoo: S&P 500, DAX, FTSE 100, CAC 40, SMI, AEX, IBEX 35, FTSE MIB, Nikkei 225,
  Hang Seng, S&P/ASX 200, S&P/TSX Composite.
- 9 currencies against the US dollar: EUR, JPY, GBP, CHF, AUD, CAD, NZD, SEK, NOK. Daily fixings from Norges Bank
  (quoted against NOK; the rate against the dollar is the ratio of two fixings taken at the same time).
- 3-month interbank rates per currency, monthly, from the OECD. A month's rate is used from the following month on.
  Japan before 2002-04 (not in the source): 0.1%. Hong Kong uses the US rate (the currency is pegged).
- Calendar: Monday to Friday. A market with no price that day keeps its last price (a zero return).
- Window: returns from 2000-02-01 to 2025-12-31. The euro starts in 1999, which sets the start. 2026 stays frozen.

RETURN OF ONE MARKET (what a futures position earns above cash)
- Currency: the day's change of the spot rate against the dollar, plus (its interest rate minus the US rate) / 252.
- Equity index: the day's change of the index, plus (dividend yield minus the local interest rate) / 252. The dividend
  yield is a flat 2.5% a year for price indices and 0 for the DAX, which already includes dividends.

SIGNALS (each computed from data up to and including day t; nothing is fitted)
- Trend, per market: the average of two signs, each +1 or -1: (a) the 4-day average of the market's cumulative return
  index against its 48-day average; (b) the return over the last 252 days.
- Carry, currencies only: rank the 9 currencies by interest rate minus the US rate; weight = rank minus the average
  rank, scaled so the absolute weights sum to 1.
- Relative momentum, within currencies and within equity indices separately: rank by the return from 252 days ago to
  21 days ago; weights as for carry.

POSITIONS
- Volatility of a market: the standard deviation of its last 60 daily returns, as a yearly figure, never below 2%.
- A sleeve's raw position in a market is its signal (trend: divided by the number of markets) divided by that
  market's volatility.
- A sleeve is scaled to 10% expected yearly volatility, using the covariance of the last 126 daily returns.
- Five sleeves: currency trend, currency carry, currency momentum, equity trend, equity momentum.
- Currency book = the average of its three sleeves, scaled to 10%. Equity book = the average of its two, scaled to
  10%. The book = the average of the two, scaled to 10%.
- Decided on the last trading day of each week (day t). Traded at the close of the next trading day (t+1), so the
  new positions earn from day t+2. Held unchanged for a week. This also covers markets that close at different hours.

COSTS
- Per side: 1.5 basis points of the amount traded for major markets, 3 for minor ones (the table marks each).
- Rolling to the next contract: 4 times a year at 1 basis point of the position.
- A doubled-cost case is reported.

PRIMARY OUTCOME
The yearly Sharpe ratio of the book's daily returns after costs, 2000-02-01 to 2025-12-31, with a 95% interval from a
circular block bootstrap (60-day blocks, 2,000 draws, seed 0).

SECONDARY OUTCOMES
- Sortino ratio, yearly return, volatility and worst drawdown of the book, of each sector book and of each sleeve.
- Each half: 2000-02 to 2012-12 and 2013-01 to 2025-12. By calendar year.
- Correlation of monthly returns with the S&P 500 and with an equal-weight basket of the 12 indices; the book's
  return in the months when that basket fell by more than 5%.
- Correlations between the five sleeves.
- Whole contracts: the same book held in whole contracts (the smallest listed contract per market, sizes in the
  table, not yet verified against the exchanges) for 1,000,000 NOK and for 100,000 NOK run at 10% volatility.
  Reported: Sharpe ratio and the share of positions that round to zero.
- Turnover per year and the share of gross return lost to costs.

TRIALS (every configuration is a trial; count them)
11, nothing else is run: 5 sleeves, 2 sector books, the book (primary), the book at doubled costs, the book in whole
contracts at two sizes. No parameter is varied.

PASS CRITERIA (all four)
1. The book's Sharpe ratio after costs is positive and its 95% interval is above 0.
2. Sharpe and Sortino ratios after costs are positive in both halves.
3. Each sector book has a positive Sharpe ratio after costs over the whole window.
4. The book's Sharpe ratio is still positive at doubled costs.

KILL IF
The book's Sharpe ratio after costs in 2013-01 to 2025-12 is zero or negative. That is the period closest to the one
it would be traded in, and the one the published studies did not see.
Between kill and pass: inconclusive.

WHAT EACH RESULT LEADS TO
- Pass: one look at the frozen 2026 window with the owner's approval, then engine support for futures and a paper book.
- Inconclusive with a positive Sharpe ratio in both halves: the owner's stated bar for a proof of concept (Sharpe and
  Sortino not negative) is met, so the owner decides whether to go on to paper; it is reported as inconclusive.
- Kill: nothing is built for this book.

KNOWN RISKS AND BIASES
- Free data. Index closes stand in for futures prices; the dividend yield is a flat assumption, which matters most
  for positions held long or short for years. A pilot needs proper futures data and this test rerun on it.
- The S&P/TSX Composite stands in for the TSX 60 future. NOK, SEK and NZD futures are thinly traded; a fund would
  more likely use forwards, at costs not measured here.
- The universe is today's list of liquid markets. All 21 were liquid in 2000, so the selection effect is small.
- Costs are assumptions, not measurements.
- Profit is earned in each contract's currency and the fund counts in NOK. That exposure is on the profit only, not
  on the position, and is ignored.
- Interest earned on the cash is not counted: all returns are above cash.
- Published strategies tend to earn less after publication. The second half is the honest read.
