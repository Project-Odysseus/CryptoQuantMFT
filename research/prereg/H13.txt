PRE-REGISTRATION H13: A SMOOTH, VOLATILITY-NORMALISED TREND SIGNAL ON THE NASDAQ-100, S&P 500 AND STOXX EUROPE 600 BEATS THE BINARY RULE ON SKEW WITHOUT LOSING ITS RETURN
Version 1. Written 2026-10-19, before any H13 test was run and before any price of the Nasdaq-100 or STOXX Europe 600 was
looked at in this project. Changes after results = a new version (lock it again), logged as extra trials.

PURPOSE
The owner asked (2026-10-19) how the trend designs in two papers would look on equity index futures (Nasdaq-100, S&P 500,
STOXX Europe 600), and for a paper book running what was found, next to the crypto books on the dashboard. Research and
paper only: the owner may not trade these markets personally (employer rule), so nothing here can lead to a live book.
The papers (local, not in git, documentation/):
- R. J. Martin, "Design and analysis of momentum trading strategies", arXiv:2101.01006v2 (2023).
- Concretum Research, "Chasing Skew in Trend Following" (2026-10-16).

PRIOR EXPOSURE
- H11 (2026-10-09) ran a trend rule (the 4/48-day average and the 252-day return, as signs) on the S&P 500 and 11 other
  indices, 2000-2025: equity trend had a Sharpe ratio of 0.01 after costs, 0.30 before 2013 and -0.27 after. So the
  S&P 500 has been seen; the Nasdaq-100 and the STOXX Europe 600 have not.
- Only the first and last dates and the row counts of the three free series were read (2026-10-19): Nasdaq-100 from
  1985-10, S&P 500 from 1970-01, STOXX Europe 600 from 2004-04-26. No price, return or chart.
- Martin's settings (speeds, the 0.6 dead zone, the highlighted activation) were chosen on 20+ years of futures across
  asset classes ending around 2012-2020, which include equity index futures and overlap this window. So the first
  half of the window is partly in-sample for the design; the second half less so.
- The 4/48-day rule (variant B4) was chosen on BTC, ETH and SOL in this project.

MARKET BEHAVIOUR
Equity indices trend for weeks to months. A trend position that grows with the strength of the trend and stays small
while the trend is weak has positively skewed returns over months; a position that is always full size (+1 or -1) does
not (Martin, section 4.2.3).

MECHANISM
Skew is a property of the design (Martin): the position is larger after the strategy has already made money, so the
big outcomes are mostly gains. Expected return needs trends to exist, which is the usual slow-diffusion and
gradual-repositioning story. The smooth activation adds no edge of its own; it is expected to keep the binary rule's
return while trading less in choppy markets and having better skew.

DATA
- Daily closes from Yahoo: ^NDX (Nasdaq-100), ^GSPC (S&P 500), ^STOXX (STOXX Europe 600, price index). Cached under
  data/research_cache/h13/ on the first run.
- 3-month interbank rates, monthly, from the OECD cache of H11 (data/research_cache/multi_asset/rates.csv): USA for the
  two US indices, EA20 for the STOXX Europe 600. A month's rate is used from the following month on.
- Calendar: Monday to Friday. A market with no price that day keeps its last price (a zero return).
- Window: returns from 2005-07-01 to 2025-12-31, the same for all three markets (the STOXX series starts 2004-04; the
  first year is warm-up for the slowest signal). 2026 stays frozen (governance.FINAL_HOLDOUT_START).
- Halves: 2005-07-01 to 2015-06-30 and 2015-07-01 to 2025-12-31.

RETURN OF ONE MARKET (what a futures position earns above cash)
The day's change of the index, plus (dividend yield minus the local 3-month rate) / 252. Flat dividend yields:
Nasdaq-100 0.9%, S&P 500 1.9%, STOXX Europe 600 3.2% a year.

SIGNALS (each computed from data up to and including day t; nothing is fitted)
- Volatility: sigma_t = square root of an exponential average (decay 1 - 1/20) of squared daily returns, floored at
  5% a year (0.05 / sqrt(252) a day).
- Volatility-adjusted return U_t = r_t / sigma_{t-1}; cumulative Y_t = sum of U up to t.
- For a speed pair (N_fast, N_slow), a = 1 - 1/N_fast, b = 1 - 1/N_slow, E_a = exponential average of Y with decay a:
  z = (E_a(Y) - E_b(Y)) / sqrt((a - b)^2 (1 + a b) / ((1 - a^2)(1 - b^2)(1 - a b))), which has unit variance when the
  U are independent with unit variance (Martin, appendix A).
- Speeds: (10, 20), (20, 40), (40, 80) days.
- Activation psi(z), each scaled so that E[psi(Z)^2] = 1 for Z standard normal (Martin's normalisation):
  P  (primary) compound sigmoid, Martin's highlighted example (figure 8): w_S = 0.30, w_R = 0.71, lambda = 0.75,
     psi = w_S c_S (2 Phi(lambda z) - 1) + w_R (1 + 2 lambda^2)^(3/4) z exp(-lambda^2 z^2 / 2),
     c_S = (2/pi arctan(lambda^2 / sqrt(1 + 2 lambda^2)))^(-1/2).
  B1 sign: psi = sign(z). The binary bet Martin argues against.
  B2 linear: psi = z, capped at +/-2.
  B3 double step: psi = c (1[z > 0.6] - 1[z < -0.6]), c = (2 Phi(-0.6))^(-1/2).
  B4 the rule the crypto and futures books run: +1 when the 4-day simple average of the close is above the 48-day one,
     -1 below. One speed only.
- A market's forecast (P, B1, B2, B3) = the average of psi over the three speeds.

POSITIONS
- Position in a market (a fraction of the book's capital as futures notional) = forecast x 0.10 / annualised sigma_t,
  divided by 3 (the three markets share the book equally), each capped at +/-1.
- Decided at the close of day t, traded at the close of t+1, earning from t+2.
- No-trade band: a market's position is traded to its target only when the two differ by more than 0.02 (the paper
  engine's rebalance band, 2% of equity).
- The book is the sum of the three. No further volatility scaling.

COSTS
- Per side: 1.5 basis points of the amount traded for the Nasdaq-100 and S&P 500, 3 for the STOXX Europe 600.
- Rolling: 4 times a year at 1 basis point of the position.
- A doubled-cost case of P is reported.

PRIMARY OUTCOME
For P and B1: the yearly Sharpe ratio of the book's daily returns after costs over the window, with a 95% interval from a
circular block bootstrap (60-day blocks, 2,000 draws, seed 0), and the skew of non-overlapping 63-day book returns.

SECONDARY OUTCOMES (all variants, the book and each market)
- Sortino ratio, yearly return, volatility, worst drawdown, each half, turnover per year, share of gross return lost to
  costs, correlation of monthly returns with the S&P 500.
- Skew of book returns at 1, 5, 21 and 63 days (non-overlapping).
- Trades (a trade = a run of days on which a market's position keeps the same sign, from entry to the day it ends):
  skew of closed-trade returns (one row per trade) and of daily returns inside trades (one row per trade-day), and the
  intra-trade skew with each trade weighted equally; share of winning trades; for trades of 20 days or more, where the
  average path peaks (as a share of the trade's life) and how much of the peak it gives back by the exit.
- The book in 2008, 2020 and 2022.

TRIALS (every configuration is a trial; count them)
21: 5 variants x (the book + 3 markets) = 20, plus P at doubled costs. No parameter is varied.

PASS CRITERIA (all three)
1. P's book Sharpe ratio after costs is positive over the window and in both halves.
2. P's Sharpe ratio is no more than 0.10 below B1's.
3. P's 63-day skew is higher than B1's.

KILL IF
P's book Sharpe ratio after costs from 2015-07-01 to 2025-12-31 is zero or negative.
Between kill and pass: inconclusive.

WHAT EACH RESULT LEADS TO
The owner asked for a paper book either way, so the paper book runs P on the micro Nasdaq-100 and micro S&P 500 futures
and the STOXX Europe 600 future at 10,000,000 NOK of pretend capital, and its config states this verdict. Pass: the
paper record is a test of a model that passed. Inconclusive or kill: the paper record is a test of the setup and of
how the design behaves, not of a model expected to earn. Never live (employer rule).

KNOWN RISKS AND BIASES
- Free data: index closes stand in for futures prices; dividend yields are flat assumptions; no real roll gaps.
- Only three markets, all equity and highly correlated: a book this narrow has few independent bets, so Sharpe
  intervals will be wide and one regime (2022) can dominate.
- H11 already showed equity trend after 2013 near zero or negative. The prior for criterion 1 is weak.
- Martin's design was chosen on data that overlaps the first half.
- Costs are assumptions, not measurements.
- Interest on cash is not counted: returns are above cash.
