# H13: Smooth trend activation on the Nasdaq-100, S&P 500 and STOXX Europe 600: report

**Verdict: PASS on all three pre-registered criteria, but a weak pass.** **The strategy card run afterwards (section 9) finds no evidence of an edge, and its timing placebo shows the return is mostly the equity market's rise, not trend timing.** The smooth design (P) has a Sharpe ratio of
0.27 after costs, against 0.31 for the ±1 rule on the same signal, and a 95% interval of -0.11 to 0.65. It has
better skew over a quarter (+0.53 against +0.33). Both halves are positive (0.38, then 0.16). Three correlated equity
markets are a narrow book, and the interval includes zero. **The result does not show that it earns money. It shows
that the smooth design costs little return and improves skew, as Martin's paper predicts.**

Development data only: 5,349 trading days, 2005-07-01 to 2025-12-31; 2026 not looked at. Reproduce:
`python scripts/research/h13_study.py --no-ledger` (logged run `data/research/h13_20261019T074922Z/`, 21 trials).
Requested by the owner on 2026-10-19 after reading R. J. Martin, "Design and analysis of momentum trading
strategies" (arXiv:2101.01006), and Concretum Research, "Chasing Skew in Trend Following" (2026-10-16). Research and
paper only: the owner may not trade these markets personally.

## 2. The book by design (after costs, each market at 10%/3 volatility)

| Design | Sharpe (95% interval) | 2005-07 to 2015-06 | 2015-07 to 2025-12 | Sortino | Worst drawdown | Turnover a year | 63-day skew |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **P** compound sigmoid | **0.27** (-0.11 to 0.65) | 0.38 | 0.16 | 0.36 | -21% | 6.7x | **+0.53** |
| B1 sign of the same signal | 0.31 (-0.09 to 0.70) | 0.45 | 0.17 | 0.42 | -20% | 6.2x | +0.33 |
| B2 linear (capped at ±2) | 0.18 | 0.29 | 0.07 | 0.23 | -28% | 6.8x | +0.79 |
| B3 double step, dead zone 0.6 | 0.24 | 0.34 | 0.14 | 0.31 | -26% | 7.4x | +0.48 |
| B4 the 4/48 average rule, ±1 | -0.05 | -0.14 | 0.03 | -0.07 | -30% | 15.8x | -0.08 |

P returned 2% a year at 9% volatility. Costs take 0.16% a year, and the Sharpe ratio is 0.25 at doubled costs.
Correlation with the S&P 500 is +0.24 by month. The long bias of equity trend makes it a partial equity exposure,
not a hedge.

## 3. Skew depends on the horizon, as both papers say

| Design | 1 day | 5 days | 21 days | 63 days |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P | -0.93 | -1.33 | -0.28 | +0.53 |
| B1 | -0.77 | -1.10 | -0.30 | +0.33 |
| B2 | -1.38 | -2.32 | -0.37 | +0.79 |
| B4 | -0.38 | -0.40 | -0.25 | -0.08 |

Every design that sizes with the signal's strength is left-skewed day to day and week to week, and right-skewed over a
quarter. This matches Martin's term structure, where skew rises to a peak at about 1.7 × (fast + slow) days, and
Concretum's futures numbers (-0.25 daily to +0.84 quarterly). The more linear the design, the stronger both tails:
B2 has the worst weekly skew and the best quarterly skew. Equity indices add their own negative daily skew (crashes),
which the papers' symmetric-return theory leaves out. **On this book a bad week is normal, and only quarters show the
designed shape.**

## 4. Trades (all three markets pooled)

A trade is a run of days on which a market's position keeps the same sign.

| Design | Trades | Winning share | Closed-trade skew | Intra-trade skew, per day / per trade | Average path peaks at | Gives back from the peak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P | 187 | 18% | +3.19 | -0.85 / -1.25 | 60% of its life | 61% |
| B1 | 193 | 25% | +2.77 | -0.68 / -0.60 | 65% | 52% |
| B3 | 190 | 28% | +3.13 | -0.84 / -0.97 | 70% | 61% |
| B4 | 629 | 31% | +2.05 | -0.29 / -0.16 | 80% | 50% |

This reproduces Concretum's finding on a different program. Closed trades are strongly right-skewed (+3.2), while
the days inside them are left-skewed. The average long trade peaks well before its exit and gives back about 60% of
the peak; Concretum found 80% and 63%. P wins only 18% of its trades, so a few long trends pay for many small losses.
That is the expected shape, and it should not count against the paper book. The commenter's concern that pooling
trade-days overweights long trades is answered: weighting each trade equally makes P's intra-trade skew more negative
(-1.25), not less.

## 7. Read

- **Martin's design claim holds out of his sample.** A smooth activation keeps most of the binary rule's return
  (0.27 against 0.31, inside the 0.10 tolerance) and has better quarterly skew, at the same turnover. The cost is a
  slightly worse daily and weekly ride. The double-step dead zone (B3) is in between. Linear (B2) has the best skew
  and the worst Sharpe ratio.
- **The rule the crypto books run (B4, 4/48 days ±1) does not work on equity indices.** It has a Sharpe ratio of
  -0.05, turns over 16 times a year and has no positive skew at any horizon. That matches H11's equity trend result.
  The slower, volatility-normalised speeds (10/20 to 40/80 days) are what make the difference here, more than the
  activation.
- **Not a model to expect money from.** The interval includes zero, the book is three correlated markets, and Europe
  has not trended for ten years. A fund would want this inside a diversified book (currencies, bonds, commodities),
  where trend earns its keep.
- **For crypto:** apply the same lesson. A test of `trend_forecast` against the 4/48 rule on BTC/ETH/SOL would need its
  own pre-registration. At the live book's one lot it cannot express sizes; it only works as a filter.

## 8. What was built

The owner asked for a paper book either way, so it runs P: `config/portfolio.equity_trend_paper.toml`, launched by
`deploy/launchd/com.cryptoquant.paper-equity-trend.plist`. The pieces are:
- `src/signals/trend_forecast.py`, the forecast;
- the `trend_forecast` strategy;
- continuous sleeves, resized every bar;
- the STOXX Europe 600 future (FXXP) in the contract table, priced at its cash index because the free source has no
  Eurex contracts.

The book holds whole micro contracts at 10,000,000 NOK, so sizes come in steps of one contract. That is about 3 MNQ,
9 MES and up to 5 FXXP at full size.

## 9. Addendum: the strategy card (2026-10-19, not pre-registered)

The strategy card (`src/research/strategy_checks.py`, standard version 1) was built after this study and run on
its saved returns. It adds no trials. Command:
`python scripts/research/strategy_card.py data/research/h13_20261019T084004Z/daily_net.csv --strategy P --baseline B1 --variants all --sweep --family H13 --positions data/research/h13_20261019T084004Z/positions.csv --market data/research/h13_20261019T084004Z/market_returns.csv`

**Verdict: no evidence of an edge; not shown better than the ±1 rule.**

| Check | Value | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mean return, Newey-West t | 1.30 | fails (needs 2) |
| Sharpe interval | 0.27 (-0.11 to 0.65) | fails |
| Deflated Sharpe (21 trials) | 0.25 | fails |
| Romano-Wolf p across the 5 designs | 0.14 | fails |
| Probability of backtest overfitting | 0.26 | passes |
| Years with a positive return | 48% of 21 | fails (needs 60%) |
| Timing placebo | p = 0.69 | **fails**: positions shifted months in time earn as much |
| Longest time under water | 8.2 years | |
| Shrunk Sharpe (Bayesian) | 0.23 ± 0.20; P(Sharpe > 0) = 0.87 | |
| P minus B1 | -0.04 (-0.12 to +0.05); P(P better than B1) = 0.20 | |

The pre-registered criteria were too lenient: they asked for a positive Sharpe, not a significant one. The timing
placebo is the important new fact. **The book's return is mostly the equity market's rise captured by being net long,
not the timing of trends.** The skew finding stands, since it is about the shape of the returns, not their mean. The
paper book keeps running as a record of the design, and its config says this.

## 10. Limits

- Free data: index closes stand in for futures prices, with flat dividend yields and no real roll gaps.
- Costs are assumptions.
- The window starts in 2005 because the STOXX series does; the first half overlaps the data Martin chose his settings
  on.
