# H9: A rank buffer on the taker basket — report

**Verdict: KILL. The buffer cut turnover by 22% and cost 5 points of return a year. The paper book keeps its rule.**
Development data only (2022-03-23 to 2025-12-31, point-in-time Kraken list, top 30, every 10 days). Reproduce:
`python scripts/research/h9_study.py --no-ledger` (logged run `data/research/h9_20261005T174917Z/`, 7 trials).

`research/prereg/H9.txt`, version 1 (e5f7d254923e), locked and committed before the test. No deviations.

## 2. Result

| | Baseline | Exit 30% | Exit 35% (primary) | Exit 45% |

At 2x costs: baseline +11.3%, buffered +7.7%. Difference in daily returns, buffered minus baseline: -5.1% a year
(95% interval -14.3% to +3.7%). By year: -7.6, -5.7, +2.7, -10.5 points.

On today's Kraken list from 2020-06 (reported only): baseline +47.4% (Sharpe 1.52), buffered +42.8% (1.50).

## 3. What this means

- The buffer saves about 1.5 points of costs a year and gives up about 6.5 points of return before costs. The coins
  it keeps (ranked between the top 20% and the top 35%) do not carry the effect: the signal sits in the extremes.
- Every buffer width is below the baseline on return, so this is not one unlucky cell.
- Drawdown does improve (25% to 18%), mostly because more coins are held per side. That is diversification, not
  better selection, and is not what was tested.
- Turnover of 51 times a year with a 10-day schedule means the basket replaces most of its coins at each
  re-rank. That is the nature of this signal; the lever on its cost is cheaper execution (the maker orders, and the
  measured Kraken spreads), not slower trading.
- Note: the baseline here (top 30, 10-day, point-in-time list, 2022-2025) has a Sharpe of 0.72, below the 1.08 the
  top-50 weekly version showed on the same universe in H6.
