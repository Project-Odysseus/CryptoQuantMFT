# H11: Trend, carry and relative momentum on equity index futures and currencies — report

**Verdict: INCONCLUSIVE. A Sharpe ratio of 0.32 after costs over 26 years, but almost all of it was earned before
2013. Since 2013 the book is flat (0.04). It meets the owner's stated bar for a proof of concept (Sharpe and Sortino
not negative in both halves) and does not meet the pre-registered pass.**
Development data only (12 equity indices, 9 currencies, 6,762 trading days, 2000-02-01 to 2025-12-31; 2026 not
looked at). Reproduce: `python scripts/research/h11_study.py --no-ledger` (logged run
`data/research/h11_20261009T101359Z/`, 11 trials).

## 1. Pre-registration and deviations

`research/prereg/H11.txt`, version 1 (1835870604f0), locked and committed before any price was looked at. No

## 2. Result

The book: half currencies (trend, carry, relative momentum), half equity indices (trend, relative momentum), run at
10% volatility, decided weekly and traded the next day. Returns are above cash and after costs.

| Sharpe ratio | 0.32 (95% interval -0.03 to 0.68) | 0.62 | 0.04 |

## 3. What the fund would care about

- **It is unrelated to equities.** Correlation of monthly returns with the S&P 500 is 0.00, and -0.04 with the
  basket of 12 indices. In the 30 months that basket fell more than 5% (average -8.0%), the book averaged +1.3%.
- **The sleeves are nearly unrelated to each other** (correlations between -0.01 and 0.33), so combining them works
  as intended. The problem is the return of each, not the mix.
- **The currency half carries it.** Currency carry is the best single sleeve. The equity half adds nothing: trend
  on equity indices lost money after 2013, and relative momentum between countries is flat in both halves.
- **Years:** best 2002 (+25.5%), 2005 (+20.8%), 2001 (+18.8%), 2008 (+16.4%), 2021 (+15.1%); worst 2011 (-18.7%),
  2015 (-15.2%), 2022 (-12.4%), 2018 (-11.7%).

## 4. Whole contracts: the budget is too small for futures

Run on 1,000,000 NOK of capital at 10% volatility, an average position is 100,000 to 300,000 NOK, and one contract
of most equity index futures is 200,000 to 2,800,000 NOK.

| Capital | Sharpe | Positions that round to zero | Correlation with the exact book |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000,000 NOK | 0.24 | 49% | 0.84 |
| 100,000 NOK | 0.25 | 93% | 0.41 |

At 100,000 NOK the book is mostly empty and what remains is a different strategy. At 1,000,000 NOK half the
positions disappear. If the 1,000,000 NOK is a **risk budget** (the volatility or loss the fund accepts) and not the
capital, the positions are about ten times larger and the rounding stops mattering. Contract sizes in
`research/data/multi_asset_markets.csv` are from memory and must be checked against the exchanges before any use.

## 5. Read

- Not a credible stand-alone model. Thirteen years of nothing is what a fund manager will see first, and it matches
  what is known: these published strategies earned much less after they became widely known.
- The one property worth something to a multi-asset fund is the lack of correlation with equities and the positive
  return in bad equity months. That is a diversification argument, not a return argument, and at a Sharpe ratio near
  zero it is weak.
- Leads, each needing its own pre-registration (none tested): the currency book alone; a slower book (the turnover
  of 57 times a year costs 1.4% and comes mostly from the fast 4/48-day rule and weekly resizing); proper futures
  data. Choosing any of these now would be choosing after seeing the result, so they count as new ideas.
