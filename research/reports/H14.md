# H14: A regime-adaptive trend + range strategy on equity index futures: report

**Outcome: D, not supported.** The pre-registered primary combination has the following results on the Nasdaq-100
(NQ proxy), 1999-2025, after costs:
- A Sharpe ratio of **0.19** (95% interval -0.16 to 0.53; t 1.11; deflated Sharpe 0.14 over 32 trials).
- That is **below** the naive static 50/50 blend (0.29), the hard switch (0.24), the exposure-matched static benchmark
  (0.21) and the trend model alone (0.27).
- It is also below the simplest filters: 20/100-day MA 0.41, 200-day MA filter 0.37.
- The overlay does not choose the better model: the timing placebo fails at p = 0.52.
- It loses to the static blend in every one of the 18 sensitivity settings, and in 4 of 7 markets.

Only one of the seven criteria passes (E7, not dependent on one year).

Run: `data/research/h14_20261019T140716Z/` (tables.md, charts/, CSVs, strategy_card/, classification.json).
Pre-registration `research/prereg/H14.txt` v1 (834807490b0e), locked and committed (4ed94eb) before any result;
config `research/h14_regime_switch.toml` v1. 145 trials logged.

## 1. Research question

Can a trend model and a range (mean-reversion) model, two economically distinct return mechanisms, be combined by
a timely causal regime overlay so that the result beats each model alone, a naive blend and a simple switch, after
costs, without being equity beta or reduced exposure?

## 2. Economic hypothesis

Index returns alternate between persistent directional moves, which a trend model earns from, and inefficient
oscillation around a slowly moving level, which a range model earns from. If market structure reveals the current
state early enough, an overlay can weight the model that fits it.

## 3. Data and execution assumptions

- **Prices.** Daily Yahoo index OHLC stands in for the futures:
  - Nasdaq-100 for NQ (the development market);
  - S&P 500 (ES), Russell 2000 (RTY), DAX, Euro Stoxx 50, Nikkei 225 and FTSE 100 for validation.
  
  There is no continuous NQ history in the free data.
- **Returns.** A rolled future earns the index return plus (flat dividend yield − local 3-month OECD rate, used a
  month late) ÷ 252. An index has no roll gaps or back-adjustment, so roll distortions cannot arise. Trade entries
  near quarterly roll days are 8.7%, against a base rate of 7.6%. The code takes any OHLC series, so real NQ
  contracts can replace the proxy.
- **Data checks** (tables.md, "Data checks"):
  - no duplicates, and no bars with the high below the open or close;
  - calendar gaps are holidays only;
  - the DAX (to 1993) and the Nikkei (to 1988) have close-only early history, so each starts the year after;
  - the Euro Stoxx 50 exists only from 2007-03.
- **Window.** 1999-01-04 to 2025-12-31: exploratory 1999–2011, validation 2012–2025, nothing fitted on either. 2026
  is frozen and was not looked at.
- **Timing.** A signal from the close of t is traded at the close of t+1 and earns from t+2; tested in
  `tests/test_regime_switch.py`.
- **Position size.** Target signal × 10% ÷ EWMA volatility, capped at 2×, with a 2% band.
- **Costs.** 1.5–3 bps per side by market, plus 4 rolls a year at 1 bp, run at ×0.5, ×1 and ×2.5.
- **Calibration.** Percentile scores are walk-forward: each year is scored against the 500 days before it and frozen
  within the year.

## 4. Signal definitions (as specified; all parameters in the config)

- **Trend model:** 0.5 × tanh((20-day MA − 100-day MA) ÷ ATR ÷ 2) + 0.5 × a 60/20-day breakout state. The rolling
  highs and lows exclude the current bar.
- **Range model:** z = (close − 40-day EWMA) ÷ the 40-day residual standard deviation, entering at |z| ≥ 1.5.
  - It stays out when the close is beyond the previous 40-day range by more than 0.5 ATR, the trend is strong
    (|trend| > 0.5), or there is a volatility breakout (10-day volatility > 1.5 × 60-day).
  - It exits when z crosses zero, at a stop 0.5 ATR beyond the boundary, on an opposite breakout, when the regime
    leaves, or after 20 days.
  - It never reverses on the same bar.
  - Version A is symmetric; version B caps shorts at 0.5.
- **Regime features:** efficiency ratio, OLS slope t-stat of log price, ADX(14), the Lo–MacKinlay 5-day variance
  ratio, the 10/60-day volatility ratio, and the distance beyond the 60-day breakout in ATRs. A rolling ADF on the
  residual is a diagnostic only.

## 5. Regime-overlay design and transition logic (as specified)

- **Trend probability:** 0.25 ER + 0.25 slope + 0.20 ADX + 0.15 variance ratio + 0.15 breakout, each a walk-forward
  percentile.
- **Entering a regime:** TREND at ≥ 0.65, RANGE at ≤ 0.35, each after 3 confirmations. Only 2 confirmations are
  needed when volatility expands with directional confirmation; volatility expansion without direction gives
  TRANSITION.
- **Leaving a regime:** after 2 closes beyond 0.50. A move to the opposite regime always goes through TRANSITION.
- **Rapid breakout:** a break ≥ 0.5 ATR beyond the 60-day range, with expanding volatility and direction, moves RANGE
  to TRANSITION at once and closes a range position on the wrong side.
- **Weights:**
  - RANGE: 1 to the range model; TREND: 1 to the trend model; neutral TRANSITION: 0.25 / 0.25.
  - From RANGE to TREND: 0.25/0.25, then 0/0.5, then 0/1.
  - From TREND to RANGE: 0.5/0, then 0.25/0.25, then 1/0.
  - In TRANSITION, gross model exposure is capped at 0.5.
- **Stress** (10-day volatility above its 95th percentile, or a gap over 3 ATR): the signal is halved, no new range
  entries for 3 days, and no step larger than 0.5.
- **Dead zone:** |signal| < 0.10 gives zero.

Where the specification left a point open, the pre-registration fixed it before any result: what counts as a strong
trend, the volatility veto, conflicting components, directional confirmation, and the 100-day slow MA.

## 7. Regime diagnostics (tables.md, "regimes", "Sharpe of each model on the days...")

- **Time in each state:** RANGE 36%, TRANSITION 30%, TREND 34%, STRESS 9% of days. There are **12.2 regime changes a
  year**; RANGE and TREND last about 23–25 days at the median, TRANSITION 8.
- **The overlay puts the models in the wrong places.**

  | Model | Sharpe in RANGE | in TRANSITION | in TREND |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Trend model | −0.22 | **+0.57** | +0.34 |
  | Range model | −0.01 | −0.32 | −0.02 |

  The trend model earns the most in TRANSITION, exactly where the overlay cuts it to a quarter or half. The range
  model doesn't earn in RANGE, its supposed home.
- **Regime changes cost money.** In the 10 days after a regime change, the primary averages −0.2 bps while the trend
  model alone earns +4.9 bps.
- **False switches.** In 48 of 79 TREND episodes the trend model lost, for −70% of capital in total. Episodes shorter
  than 5 days cost the primary −12%.
- **Delayed trend recognition.** After a new 60-day breakout, full trend weight takes a median of 13 days, and is
  never reached within 60 days in 17 of 101 breakouts. The trend probability barely moves after a breakout (0.52 on
  day 0, 0.56 on day 9). The rapid-breakout exception adds 0.004 Sharpe.
- **Switching turnover.** 3.3 of the 7.8 turnover a year comes on the day after a weight change. Without switching
  costs the Sharpe would be 0.20.
- **TRANSITION** covers 30% of days, while the market returned 4.2% a year on those days:
  - the primary earned 0.0% a year in it, against 1.4% for the trend model;
  - reduced exposure there cost 0.6% a year against the static blend.

## 8. Robustness (tables.md "sensitivity", "feature ablation", "simple filters")

- **Sensitivity.** All 18 settings have positive net returns, with Sharpe from 0.16 to 0.27. In every one, the primary
  is below the static 50/50 blend at the same setting. It is a plateau, but a plateau beneath the simpler alternative.
- **Feature ablation.** Each feature alone does as well as or better than the full overlay: slope alone 0.33,
  breakout alone 0.32, ER alone 0.29, against 0.19. Removing ADX helps (0.25).
  - Features are redundant: ER and the slope t-stat have a rank correlation of 0.77, and ER and breakout 0.55.
  - No feature tells which model will earn more over the next 20 days (rank correlations −0.09 to 0.07).
- **Simple filters.** The 20/100-day-MA filter (0.41) and the 200-day MA (0.37) beat the overlay. ER above its median
  (0.21), ADX > 20 (0.16) and ADX > 25 (0.10) do not. Confidence scaling inside the models, with weight equal to the
  probability, gives 0.25: better than the state machine.

## 11. Conclusion

What each kind of statement here rests on:

- **Fixed before inspecting results:** every rule, parameter and outcome criterion (the pre-registration and the
  config).
- **Found in the data:**
  - The range model has no edge on the Nasdaq-100 in any period, or in 4 of 7 markets. Mean reversion around a
    40-day anchor does not pay in these indices at daily frequency.
  - The overlay cuts the trend model where it earns the most (TRANSITION) and switches about 12 times a year at
    a cost.
  - Its features are largely one directional measure counted several times.
- **What looks stable:** the plateau of positive net returns across the 18 settings (Sharpe 0.16–0.27), and the
  ranking against simpler alternatives at every cost level.
- **What is parameter-sensitive:** nothing changes the conclusion. The largest effects are the slower regime horizon
  (60 days: 0.27) and the 40/200 MA pair (0.26), both still below their static blends.
- **Driven by equity beta:** most of what the primary earns. Its +0.17 average net long position explains more than
  its total return.
- **Driven by exposure reduction:** the risk profile (−17% drawdown against −84% for buy-and-hold) comes from holding
  less. The exposure-matched static benchmark shows the model selection adds nothing on top.
- **Driven by a few events:** 2017 is the best year; without it the Sharpe falls to 0.12.

**The overlay does not identify regimes in a way that helps.** In-sample differences between the ex-post regime
labels are not evidence of predictability, and here even those differences point the wrong way.

## 12. Post-hoc observations (not tested; each would be a new pre-registered version)

- **The smallest economically motivated change is to drop the range model.** It is the component with negative
  standalone returns on the development market. That abandons the two-mechanism hypothesis, so it would be a
  different study (the H11/H13 trend studies already cover trend alone).
- **If the two-model idea is kept:**
  - the range model would need a different mechanism, such as intraday or weekly-horizon mean reversion, or relative
    value between indices, where mean reversion is better documented than in the outright index level;
  - the overlay should be one feature, not a blend of five correlated ones.
- **The strongest simple results are long-biased** (volatility-managed long, MA-filtered long). Those are equity beta
  management, not regime selection, and the owner may not trade these markets in any case.

## 13. Recommended next tests

1. **Real futures data.** Rerun the unchanged rules on NQ/ES continuous contracts with held-contract returns, to rule
   out proxy effects (not expected to change the conclusion).
2. **A pre-registered test of mean reversion alone** at shorter horizons (1–5 days) on the same indices, before any
   regime work.
3. **If regime work continues:** a single-feature overlay (slope or breakout) with a pre-registered threshold, judged
   against the static blend on the paired test, not on point Sharpe.

## 15. Limits

- Index proxies, flat dividend yields, and assumed costs.
- One development market. The cross-markets are highly correlated with each other and with it, so 27 years give few
  independent regime episodes.
- The study tests the owner's specification, not every possible regime overlay.
